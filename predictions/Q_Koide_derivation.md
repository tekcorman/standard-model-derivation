### Step 2: Ramanujan subspace of B(P) with C_3 multiplicities (4, 2, 2)

By ../predictions/B_P_doubly_degenerate_h_derivation.md Step 3 (and docs/theorem_B5_3_core.md), at the P-point k = (1/4, 1/4, 1/4) in primitive reduced coordinates of the bcc primitive Brillouin zone, the Hashimoto Bloch operator B(P) on the 12-dimensional directed-edge fibre has an 8-dimensional Ramanujan subspace V_Ram (the complement of the +/-1 tree-eigenvalue subspace) which decomposes under the body-diagonal C_3 subgroup of the 432 point group of space group I4_132 as

    V_Ram = 4 * trivial + 2 * omega + 2 * omega^2                         (*)

where omega = exp(2 pi i / 3) indexes the non-trivial C_3 characters and the integer coefficients are the isotypic multiplicities. The sum 4 + 2 + 2 = 8 matches dim V_Ram. This decomposition is a theorem upstream of the present file.

### Step 3: Substrate amplitudes on V_Ram via Jaynes max-entropy (A2 + ADOPTED-P1)

Under ADOPTED-P1, the constraint on the substrate state is "supported on V_Ram." By Jaynes 1957 (max-entropy principle) under A2 (MDL), among Hilbert-space pure states consistent with this support constraint the MDL-selected state is the uniform superposition

    |psi_Ram> = (1 / sqrt(8)) * sum_{b in basis of V_Ram} |b>.

The orthogonal projector P_alpha onto the alpha-th isotypic component of (*) has rank mu_alpha, so the norm of the isotypic projection is

    || P_alpha |psi_Ram> || = sqrt(mu_alpha / 8).

The overall 1/sqrt(8) is a state normalization that cancels in the Koide ratio Q. The physically relevant substrate amplitude per isotypic component is therefore

    c_alpha = sqrt(mu_alpha),   alpha in {trivial, omega, omega^2}.

With the (4, 2, 2) multiplicities: c_trivial = sqrt(4) = 2, c_omega = sqrt(2), c_omega_bar = sqrt(2). These are REAL non-negative substrate amplitudes indexed by C_3 isotypic label.

### Step 5: Born rule (A3 + CDP 2011 Theorem 25) + Yukawa-amplitude adoption

By predictions/observer_hilbert_space.py (G.1 + G.5 closed under A1 + A2-T + A3-T), CDP 2011 Theorem 25 forces the observer's state space to be density operators on a finite-dim complex Hilbert space, with observable probabilities given by the Born rule

    p(i) = Tr(rho E_i).

For a pure state rho = |psi><psi| and rank-1 effect E_i = |e_i><e_i|, the Born rule reduces to

    p(i) = |<e_i | psi>|^2.

Applied to the generation-indexed amplitudes amp_j of Step 4, the Born-rule squared-modulus reading gives

    m_j = |amp_j|^2.                                                       (***)

ADOPTED-Y (Yukawa-amplitude identification): we adopt as a structural postulate that the substrate amplitudes amp_j are read as Yukawa-coupling amplitudes, so that Yukawa_j^2 = m_j is the mass-like quantity entering the Koide ratio. This is parallel to the I-Feshbach adoption of ../predictions/Feshbach_coupling_strength_derivation.md Section 3 (NB walk survival factor = physical coupling strength) and parallel to the mass-as-flux framing of an internal working note (mass as a Born-compatible rate rather than a bare Hamiltonian parameter). A candidate future-closure path is the Higgs-mechanism + Yukawa-coupling derivation envisioned in an internal working note; this closure is not carried out here.

Evaluating (***) with the Step 4 amplitudes:

    m_0 = (2 + 2 sqrt(2))^2 = 12 + 8 sqrt(2)
    m_1 = (2 - sqrt(2))^2   = 6 - 4 sqrt(2)
    m_2 = (2 - sqrt(2))^2   = 6 - 4 sqrt(2).

For these real non-negative amp_j, sqrt(m_j) = amp_j, so sqrt(m_0) = 2 + 2 sqrt(2) and sqrt(m_1) = sqrt(m_2) = 2 - sqrt(2).

Gate-clear at this step: A3 (axiom) + CDP 2011 Theorem 25 (cited theorem) + predictions/observer_hilbert_space.py (upstream closed) + ADOPTED-Y (flagged adopted postulate) + explicit algebra.

### Step 6: Koide-ratio closed-form evaluation

    sum_j m_j = (12 + 8 sqrt(2)) + 2 * (6 - 4 sqrt(2))
              = 12 + 8 sqrt(2) + 12 - 8 sqrt(2)
              = 24.

    sum_j sqrt(m_j) = (2 + 2 sqrt(2)) + 2 * (2 - sqrt(2))
                    = 2 + 2 sqrt(2) + 4 - 2 sqrt(2)
                    = 6.

    Q = sum_j m_j / (sum_j sqrt(m_j))^2 = 24 / 36 = 2/3.

Gate-clear: explicit algebra, sympy-verified in predictions/Q_Koide.py via implementation_sympy_Q(). The assertion sp.simplify(Q - sp.Rational(2, 3)) == 0 holds.
