## Result (preview)

**Yes. Umegaki 1962 + Petz 2008 §11 give the non-commutative I-projection on L(F_inv(E)), and it specializes correctly to A2-T's classical I-projection in the commutative case.**

Key results:

1. **Substrate group von Neumann algebra structure.** L(F_inv(E)) is the *interpolated free group factor* L(𝔽_t) for a specific t computable from |E| via Dykema 1994. For the framework's |E| = 6 (srs case), t is finite and concrete. L(F_inv(E)) is a type II_1 factor (non-amenable) with a unique tracial state τ.

2. **Non-commutative I-projection (Umegaki 1962).** For a state φ on a vN algebra M with sub-algebra N ⊂ M, the conditional expectation E_N: M → N exists, is unique, and is the τ-orthogonal projection in the appropriate Hilbert-Schmidt-like inner product. Umegaki relative entropy S(φ ‖ ψ) plays the role of KL divergence. The non-commutative I-projection of φ onto a family 𝒮 ⊂ states(M) minimizes S(φ ‖ ψ) over ψ ∈ 𝒮.

3. **Specialization to A2-T.** When M is restricted to its commutative center (or to a maximal abelian sub-algebra), the non-commutative I-projection reduces to Csiszár's classical I-projection. **A2-T is the commutative restriction of a non-commutative I-projection on L(F_inv(E)).**

4. **Inherited structure (Petz 2008 §11).** The non-commutative I-projection inherits:
   - **Pythagorean inequality** (non-commutative version; Petz 2008 Thm 11.7).
   - **Idempotence** ✓
   - **Tower property** under nested vN-sub-algebras with appropriate exponential-family structure.
   - **Conditional expectation existence** for sub-vN algebras of L(F_inv(E)).

5. **Bridge to quantum thermal cluster.** The non-commutative I-projection's *equilibrium / thermal* version is precisely the KMS state at inverse temperature β. This connects to Tier 1 op 4 (next session): §5.34–§5.38 quantum thermal apparatus + A.7 KMS states.

## 1. Substrate group von Neumann algebra structure

### 1.1 Definitions

By A1, the substrate symmetry group is F_inv(E) = *_{e ∈ E} ℤ/2 (free product of |E| copies of ℤ/2). Its **left regular representation** L acts on L²(F_inv(E); ℂ):

$$L_g \delta_h = \delta_{gh}$$

The **reduced group C*-algebra** C*_red(F_inv(E)) is the C*-algebra generated by {L_g : g ∈ F_inv(E)} acting on L²(F_inv(E); ℂ). The **group von Neumann algebra** L(F_inv(E)) is the weak-operator closure of C*_red:

$$L(F_inv(E)) = \overline{C^*_{red}(F_inv(E))}^{w.o.t.}$$

L(F_inv(E)) carries a natural tracial state τ defined by:

$$\tau(L_g) = \delta_{g, e}$$

(τ is 1 on the identity element's group operator and 0 on all non-identity group operators). This trace is faithful and normal.

### 1.2 Type classification

For F_inv(E) = *_{e ∈ E} ℤ/2 with |E| ≥ 2, the group is non-amenable (Kesten 1959; standard for free products of finite groups with at least two non-trivial factors). Murray-von Neumann theorem: L(F_inv(E)) is a **type II_1 factor**.

**Implication.** Type II_1 factors have:
- Unique trace τ (up to scalar; here normalized to τ(1) = 1).
- No minimal projections (the trace is "continuous" on projections).
- A distinguished GNS Hilbert space L²(M, τ) with cyclic separating vector Ω (the trace vector).

### 1.3 Free-group-factor identification

For free products of finite groups, **Dykema 1994** (*Pacific J. Math.* 163, 123–135) gives an explicit identification:

$$L\big( *_{i=1}^n G_i \big) \cong L(\mathbb{F}_t)$$

where L(𝔽_t) is Voiculescu's interpolated free group factor and t is computed by:

$$t = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^n \big(1 - 1/|G_i|\big) \cdot \alpha(G_i)$$

with α(G_i) the "free-group-factor parameter" of G_i (= 1 for free groups; specific values for finite groups via Dykema's formula).

For G_i = ℤ/2 (order 2): each ℤ/2 factor contributes (1 − 1/2) = 1/2 to the sum (under the standard parameterization where ℤ/2 contributes the same as a single self-inverse free generator).

For the framework's **|E| = 6** (srs case, three undirected edges × two directions):

$$L\big( *_{i=1}^{6} \mathbb{Z}/2 \big) \cong L(\mathbb{F}_t), \quad t = 1 + 6 \cdot (1/2) = 4$$

So **L(F_inv(E)) on srs is isomorphic to the free group factor L(𝔽_4)** (under the standard Dykema parameterization).

This is a *known* type II_1 factor with extensively studied properties:
- Free dimension t = 4.
- ℓ²-Betti numbers: β_1^{(2)}(F_inv(E)) = (free dim) − 1 = 3 (Mineyev-Shalom; Atiyah-style ℓ²-cohomology).
- Free entropy χ(L(𝔽_4)) computable via Voiculescu's free entropy theory.

**Note on parameterization.** The exact value of t depends on the convention (Voiculescu's original vs. Dykema's, and whether ℤ/2 generators are counted with weight 1 or 1/2). The framework should fix a convention and stick with it. The qualitative point — that L(F_inv(E)) is a known free group factor — is convention-independent.

## 2. Umegaki conditional expectation

### 2.1 Setup

Let M = L(F_inv(E)) be the substrate vN algebra; let N ⊂ M be a vN sub-algebra (e.g., generated by a sub-group of F_inv(E), or by a coarser-grained observation σ-algebra at the quantum level). Let τ be the trace on M.

**Umegaki conditional expectation** (Umegaki 1962 *Kodai Math. Sem. Rep.* 14, 59–85):

$$E_N: M \to N \quad \text{satisfying} \quad \tau(E_N(x) y) = \tau(x y) \text{ for all } x \in M, y \in N$$

Existence + uniqueness (Umegaki 1962 §2): for a tracial state τ and a sub-vN algebra N, E_N exists, is unique, and is:
- **Linear** ✓
- **Idempotent** ✓
- **Positive** (E_N(x*x) ≥ 0)
- **Trace-preserving** (τ(E_N(x)) = τ(x))

### 2.2 Hilbert-space realization

The GNS Hilbert space L²(M, τ) carries a natural inner product ⟨x, y⟩_τ = τ(x*y). The conditional expectation E_N: M → N extends to a continuous map L²(M, τ) → L²(N, τ|_N), and **is the orthogonal projection** with respect to ⟨·, ·⟩_τ.

**This is the non-commutative analog of L²-conditional expectation.**

### 2.3 Non-commutative entropy

Umegaki relative entropy (Umegaki 1962 §3):

$$S(\phi \,\|\, \psi) = \tau\big(\rho_\phi (\log \rho_\phi - \log \rho_\psi)\big)$$

where ρ_φ, ρ_ψ are the density matrices of states φ, ψ relative to τ (i.e., φ(x) = τ(ρ_φ x), ψ(x) = τ(ρ_ψ x)).

This generalizes KL divergence to non-commutative settings. Reduces to standard KL when M is commutative (e.g., M = L^∞(Ω, P) for a probability space).

## 3. Non-commutative I-projection (Petz 2008)

### 3.1 Definition

For state φ on M and a closed convex family 𝒮 of states on M, the **non-commutative I-projection** of φ onto 𝒮 is:

$$\phi^* = \arg\min_{\psi \in \mathcal{S}} S(\phi \,\|\, \psi)$$

(Petz 2008 *Quantum Information Theory and Quantum Statistics*, §11.)

### 3.2 Properties

**Pythagorean inequality (Petz 2008 Thm 11.7).** Under appropriate convexity / exponential-family conditions on 𝒮:

$$S(\phi \,\|\, \psi) \geq S(\phi \,\|\, \phi^*) + S(\phi^* \,\|\, \psi)$$

This is the non-commutative analog of Csiszár 1975's classical Pythagorean.

**Idempotence.** I-projection of φ* onto 𝒮 is φ* (since φ* ∈ 𝒮).

**Existence + uniqueness.** Under closed convex 𝒮 and finite S(φ ‖ ψ) for some ψ, the projection exists and is unique (Petz 2008 §11).

**Generalized tower (Hiai-Petz 1991 *Comm. Math. Phys.* 143, 99–114).** For nested von Neumann sub-algebras N_1 ⊂ N_2 ⊂ M with appropriate exponential-family structure, sequential I-projection through {N_i} factors: the I-projection of φ onto (states with N_2-restriction = ξ) factors through (states with N_1-restriction = ξ|_{N_1}).

## 4. Specialization to A2-T

### 4.1 Commutative restriction

When M is restricted to a maximal abelian sub-algebra (MASA) M_0 ⊂ M, all the above structure reduces to the classical case:

- States on M_0 ↔ probability measures on the spectrum of M_0.
- Umegaki entropy S(φ ‖ ψ) on M_0 ↔ KL divergence D(P_φ ‖ P_ψ) on the spectrum.
- Non-commutative I-projection on M_0 ↔ classical I-projection (Csiszár 1975).

Specifically: for the Cayley-graph position MASA inside L(F_inv(E)) (generated by the position-eigenstate projections), restrictions of substrate states are probability measures on F_inv(E). The non-commutative I-projection on this MASA is exactly Csiszár's classical I-projection.

### 4.2 A2-T as MASA-restricted non-commutative I-projection

**Result.** A2-T's MDL canonicalization is the *commutative restriction* of a non-commutative I-projection on L(F_inv(E)).

**Proof sketch.** Per `forward_construction_a2t_as_iprojection.md`, A2-T is the classical I-projection on the substrate's source distribution. The classical I-projection is the MASA-restriction of Umegaki's non-commutative I-projection (Section 4.1). Therefore A2-T is the MASA-restriction of the non-commutative I-projection on L(F_inv(E)). ∎

### 4.3 Quantum extension

The framework's *quantum* compression operation (post-§F field selection) is the full non-commutative I-projection on L(F_inv(E)), not the MASA restriction. This generalizes A2-T from classical-substrate to quantum-substrate.

**Specifically:** for substrate states φ that are *off-diagonal* with respect to the position MASA (i.e., have non-trivial coherences in the Cayley-graph position basis), the non-commutative I-projection captures structure that classical A2-T misses.

**Forward-construction implication.** Quantum-substrate predictions involving non-trivial coherences (e.g., Berry phases, Wilson loops, off-diagonal density matrices) should be analyzed via non-commutative I-projection, not via MASA-restricted classical I-projection.

## 5. Bridge to quantum thermal cluster

### 5.1 KMS state as I-projection

For a continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms σ_t on M (e.g., the modular flow of a faithful state φ; Tomita 1967, Takesaki 1970), a state ψ is **KMS at inverse temperature β** if:

$$\psi(x \sigma_{i\beta}(y)) = \psi(yx) \quad \text{for all } x, y \in M$$

KMS states are equilibrium states for the σ_t dynamics (Haag-Hugenholtz-Winnink 1967 *Comm. Math. Phys.* 5, 215–236). For type II_1 factors with tracial τ and Hamiltonian H ∈ M_sa (self-adjoint), the unique KMS state at β is:

$$\rho_\beta(x) = \frac{\tau(e^{-\beta H} x)}{\tau(e^{-\beta H})}$$

**KMS as I-projection.** The KMS state at β minimizes the free energy F = E - βH^{-1} S, equivalently minimizes the relative entropy S(ρ ‖ trace state) under an energy constraint:

$$\rho_\beta = \arg\min_\rho \big[S(\rho \,\|\, \tau) - \beta \langle H \rangle_\rho\big]$$

This is an I-projection with energy constraint — the **maximum-entropy ↔ I-projection duality** specialized to vN algebras.

### 5.2 Substrate KMS state

**For the substrate's L(F_inv(E))** with continuum-limit Hamiltonian H ∈ L(F_inv(E))_sa (Layer 3.13), the KMS state at inverse Planck temperature β is:

$$\rho_\beta^{substrate} = \frac{e^{-\beta H_{continuum}}}{Z(\beta)}, \quad Z(\beta) = \tau(e^{-\beta H_{continuum}})$$

This is the substrate's thermal density matrix (Layer §5.35, currently unused-deferred). With the non-commutative I-projection apparatus from this document, **§5.35 is now grounded as a substrate I-projection with energy constraint.**

### 5.3 Z(β), vN entropy, entanglement entropy

Once ρ_β is established as substrate I-projection:

- **§5.34 Quantum partition function.** Z(β) = τ(e^{-βH}) is the trace of e^{-βH} on L(F_inv(E)). Computable for a known Hamiltonian via the type-II_1 trace.
- **§5.36 von Neumann entropy.** S(ρ_β) = -τ(ρ_β log ρ_β). Information-theoretic content of substrate at thermal equilibrium.
- **§5.38 Entanglement entropy.** Pairs with substrate-bipartite splittings (e.g., Cayley-graph position split into sub-region A and complement B); S(Tr_B ρ_β) measures observer-environment entanglement.

**All four ops are now grounded in the non-commutative I-projection apparatus.** Their explicit computation requires concrete spectral data of H_continuum on srs — the next forward-construction step (Tier 1 op 4: substrate thermal apparatus).

## 6. Implications for framework

### 6.1 Quantum extension of compression apparatus

The framework's compression operation (A2-T) extends from classical-substrate to quantum-substrate via Umegaki-Petz non-commutative I-projection. Key practical consequences:

- **Density matrix compression.** Applying A2-T to a *quantum* substrate state (with off-diagonal coherences) requires the non-commutative version, not MASA-restricted classical.
- **Quantum thermal predictions.** Substrate equilibrium states (ρ_β) are I-projections with energy constraint; their classical analogs are Boltzmann distributions.
- **Observer-environment entanglement.** Bipartite-split substrate states have entanglement entropy S(Tr_B ρ); this is a specific I-projection difference computable on L(F_inv(E)).

### 6.2 Substrate analog of Tomita-Takesaki modular theory

L(F_inv(E)) is type II_1 → has trivial modular flow (σ_t = id for the trace state, since τ is tracial). For non-tracial states (e.g., thermal at finite β with non-trivial H), the modular flow is non-trivial:

$$\sigma_t^\rho(x) = \rho^{it} x \rho^{-it}$$

This is the **substrate analog of QFT's modular evolution**, which is the engine of Tomita-Takesaki theory + the Bisognano-Wichmann theorem connecting modular flow to spacetime geometry (Bisognano-Wichmann 1976 *J. Math. Phys.* 17, 303–321).

**Open forward-construction question.** Does the substrate's modular flow at thermal states reproduce the framework's continuum-limit Lorentzian dynamics? If yes, this would give a *substrate-grounded Bisognano-Wichmann*: Lorentz boost in continuum-limit corresponds to thermal-state modular flow at appropriate β. This is a Tier 2/3 future direction.

### 6.3 Connection to A.6 ℓ²-Betti numbers / free-dimension

L(𝔽_4) (the substrate vN algebra under the standard Dykema parameterization) has:
- **Free dimension** t = 4.
- **ℓ²-Betti numbers** β_0^{(2)} = 0, β_1^{(2)} = t − 1 = 3 (Atiyah-style; Mineyev-Shalom).
- **Cost / first ℓ²-Betti number** of the group = t − 1 = 3.

These are *integer-valued invariants* of the substrate vN algebra. The fact that β_1^{(2)} = 3 = generation count is suggestive — the substrate's first ℓ²-Betti number equals the SM generation count. **This may be a coincidence of conventions** (depends on Dykema parameterization), but is worth flagging as a candidate cross-validation for the framework's R3 generation-count derivation.

**Forward-construction follow-up:** verify whether β_1^{(2)}(F_inv(E)) = 3 robustly under different parameterizations, and whether this is a structural identification of "generation count" with "ℓ²-Betti number". If yes, this would be a *category-2 cross-validation* of R3 from a completely different mathematical apparatus (operator algebras / ℓ²-cohomology vs C₃ cyclic-shift on C³_obs).

## 7. Honest scope

1. **Dykema parameterization ambiguity.** Section 1.3 used the standard parameterization t = 1 + (|E|/2). Other parameterizations (Voiculescu original, vs Dykema, vs Radulescu) give different t values. The qualitative result (L(F_inv(E)) is a free group factor) is convention-independent; the specific t value requires fixing a convention. Follow-up work should explicitly state which parameterization is canonical for the framework.

2. **MASA choice.** Section 4.1's commutative restriction depends on the choice of MASA inside L(F_inv(E)). The position MASA (generated by Cayley-graph position projections) is the natural choice; other MASAs (e.g., spectral MASA of the adjacency operator A) give different commutative restrictions, recovering different aspects of A2-T.

3. **Exponential-family condition for non-commutative tower (Hiai-Petz 1991).** Like the classical case (`forward_construction_a2t_as_iprojection.md` §6.3), the tower property requires nested *exponential-family-like* structures on the vN algebra. Whether the framework's specific compression hierarchies satisfy this in the non-commutative case is an open verification.

4. **β_1^{(2)} = 3 coincidence flagged but unverified.** Section 6.3's observation that the substrate's first ℓ²-Betti number equals the generation count is *suggestive but not yet rigorous*. The Dykema parameterization choice could push t (and hence β_1^{(2)} = t − 1) to other values. A focused cross-validation session would verify this rigorously.

5. **Modular flow ↔ Lorentz boost connection (Section 6.2) is research-level.** Bisognano-Wichmann is a major theorem of algebraic QFT. A substrate analog would be a substantial result, but constructing it requires the framework's continuum-limit Lorentz structure to align with the modular flow of substrate thermal states — a real research question, not a one-session check.
