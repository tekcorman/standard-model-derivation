## D0-i — M is a II₁ factor: G is ICC

**Claim.** Every `g ∈ G\{e}` has an **infinite** ordinary conjugacy class `{hgh^{-1} : h ∈ G}`.

**Proof (word-length growth).** First reduce to the case `g` **cyclically reduced** (first and last
syllable in distinct factors, or `|g| ≤ 1`): if `g`'s reduced word has `i_1 = i_k` (`k ≥ 2`), then
since `G_{i_1} = ℤ/2` has a unique nontrivial element, `x_1 = x_k = t_{i_1}`, and
`t_{i_1}\, g\, t_{i_1}^{-1} = x_2 x_3 \cdots x_{k-1}` (the two boundary syllables cancel against the
conjugating `t_{i_1}`), a **conjugate** of `g` with `|·| = k-2`. Iterating strictly decreases length
and terminates (finitely many steps, since it can't reach a nontrivial element of length `< 0`) at a
cyclically reduced conjugate `ĝ` of `g`. Since conjugacy classes are literally the same set,
`\text{class}(g) = \text{class}(ĝ)`, so it suffices to prove the claim for cyclically reduced `g`.

Let `g = x_1 \cdots x_k` be cyclically reduced, `k ≥ 1` (factors of first/last syllable `i_1, i_k`,
possibly equal only if `k=1`). Since there are 6 generators, choose `b ∉ \{i_1, i_k\}` (at most 2
forbidden values, ≥ 4 remain) and any `a ≠ b`. Set `h_n = (t_a t_b)^n` (reduced, length `2n`,
`n = 0,1,2,\dots`); note `h_n^{-1} = (t_b t_a)^n`. Consider `w_n = h_n\, g\, h_n^{-1}`:

- **Left junction:** last letter of `h_n` is `t_b`; first syllable of `g` is `t_{i_1}`; `b ≠ i_1` ⟹ no
  cancellation.
- **Right junction:** last syllable of `g` is `t_{i_k}`; first letter of `h_n^{-1}` is `t_b`;
  `b ≠ i_k` ⟹ no cancellation.
- Internally, `h_n` and `h_n^{-1}` are alternating words in two distinct generators (`a ≠ b`), hence
  already reduced, and neither end touches the far boundary of `w_n` (the leftmost letter of `h_n` and
  the rightmost letter of `h_n^{-1}` sit at the very ends of `w_n`, with nothing beyond to cancel
  against).

So `w_n` is reduced **exactly as written**, with `|w_n| = 2n + k + 2n = 4n + k` — an explicit,
exact formula, not merely a lower bound. Since `n \mapsto 4n+k` is injective, the `w_n` (`n=0,1,2,\dots`)
are **pairwise distinct** elements of `\text{class}(g)`, which is therefore infinite. `∎`

Since `g ≠ e` was arbitrary, `G` is **ICC**. `G` is also visibly countably infinite (generated by 6
elements; the construction above already exhibits infinitely many distinct elements). By the standard
group–von Neumann-algebra fact (Murray–von Neumann: for a countable discrete group `Γ`, `L(Γ)` is a
factor iff `Γ` is ICC, and is then type II₁ via the faithful normal trace `τ(λ_g)=δ_{g,e}`), **`M = L(G)`
is a type II₁ factor.**

**On the excluded case.** The freeze flags that `F_inv(2) = ℤ/2 * ℤ/2 = D_∞` is the standard named
exception to ICC for free products, and that the `|E|=2` toy in
`m1b_observer_substrate_iprojection_attempt.py:331–354` (which asserts `L(D_∞) ≅ R`) sits exactly in
this degenerate case. This proof independently confirms `D_∞` really is degenerate and the toy should

`\{r, r^{-1}\}` — **finite** (conjugating by `a`: `a(ab)a^{-1} = (aa)ba \cdot a^{-1}`... concretely
`a\,r\,a = a(ab)a = (a a)(b a) = ba = r^{-1}`, and conjugating by `b` similarly returns `r` or
`r^{-1}`). So `D_∞` is **not** ICC, `L(D_∞)` has nontrivial center, and it is **not even a factor** in

## D0-ii — σ̂ ∉ Inn(G)

**Claim.** `t_1` and `t_2` are **never conjugate** in `G` (generators of distinct free factors are
never conjugate), hence `σ̂` (which sends `t_1 ↦ t_2`) is not an inner automorphism of `G`.

**Proof.** By the free-product conjugacy classification (Magnus–Karrass–Solitar Thm 4.2 / Lyndon–Schupp
Ch. IV, Thm 2.7 — the same citation family already used correctly in
`m1b_observer_substrate_iprojection_attempt.py:159–163` for a related but distinct claim): a nontrivial
element `g` with cyclically-reduced syllable length 1, i.e. `g ∈ G_i\{e\}` for a **unique** `i` (unique
because the factors intersect trivially in a free product), is conjugate **only** to elements whose own
cyclically-reduced form is again a single syllable **in the same factor `G_i`**. Concretely, this is the
`k=1` case of the very peeling argument used in D0-i: if `h g h^{-1}` (any `h ∈ G`) is to cyclically
reduce back down to syllable length 1, the peeling process (iteratively cancelling matched boundary
pairs) can only ever land in `G_i`, never in a different factor `G_j` (`j ≠ i`) — the reduction only
removes matched pairs `t_{i}, t_i` at the two ends, so the "surviving core" it uncovers is always either
`g` itself or (trivially, since `G_i ≅ ℤ/2` is abelian) `g` again.

Since `t_1 ∈ G_1` and `t_2 ∈ G_2` with `1 ≠ 2`, they are **not conjugate** in `G`.

If `σ̂ = \mathrm{Ad}(g_0)` for some `g_0 ∈ G` (i.e. `σ̂` inner), then `σ̂(t_1) = g_0 t_1 g_0^{-1}` would
lie in the conjugacy class of `t_1`. But `σ̂(t_1) = t_2` (since `σ(1)=2`), and `t_2 \notin
\text{class}(t_1)` — contradiction. **Hence `σ̂ ∉ \mathrm{Inn}(G)`.** `∎`

This argument is decisive and elementary, and — unlike the earlier `m1b_observer...` §3 sub-case 3a
(which used the fact that `G` has trivial center and no order-3 elements) — it uses the **specific
action of σ̂ on generators**, which is exactly what the freeze asks for ("bulletproof, elementary...
uses THIS α specifically").

## D0-iii — group-outer ⟹ properly outer on M (the load-bearing link)

This is the step M1.B skipped (its sub-case 3b re-cited Voiculescu's `\mathrm{Out}(F_n)
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{Out}(L(F_n))` **group-embedding** result, which says nothing about whether
*this specific* generator-permuting automorphism is outer — see the freeze's contamination note). We
prove it **self-contained**, via σ̂-twisted conjugation and an ℓ² summability argument.

### Setup: the Fourier expansion and the twisted-conjugacy identity

Suppose, for contradiction, `α = \mathrm{Ad}(u)` for a unitary `u \in M`. Expand `u` in the orthonormal
basis `\{λ_h\}_{h \in G}` of `L^2(M,\tau) \cong \ell^2(G)`:
`u = \sum_{h \in G} c_h \lambda_h`, `\sum_h |c_h|^2 = \|u\|_2^2 = 1`.

`\alpha(\lambda_g) = u \lambda_g u^*` for all `g`. Multiplying both sides on the right by `u`:
`u \lambda_g = \lambda_{\hat\sigma(g)}\, u`. Comparing the coefficient of `λ_k` on both sides (standard
Fourier-coefficient matching in `\ell^2(G)`, using `\lambda_h \lambda_g = \lambda_{hg}`):

- LHS coefficient of `λ_k`: `u\lambda_g = \sum_h c_h \lambda_{hg}`, so setting `hg=k` (`h=kg^{-1}`)
  gives coefficient `c_{kg^{-1}}`.
- RHS coefficient of `λ_k`: `\lambda_{\hat\sigma(g)}u = \sum_h c_h \lambda_{\hat\sigma(g)h}`, so setting
  `\hat\sigma(g)h=k` (`h=\hat\sigma(g)^{-1}k`) gives coefficient `c_{\hat\sigma(g)^{-1}k}`.

Equate: `c_{kg^{-1}} = c_{\hat\sigma(g)^{-1}k}` for **all** `g,k \in G`. Substituting `h := kg^{-1}`
(so `k = hg`) and then `g \to g^{-1}`:

`c_h = c_{\hat\sigma(g^{-1})^{-1} h g^{-1}} = c_{\hat\sigma(g)\, h\, g^{-1}}` for **all** `g, h \in G`.

Define the **σ̂-twisted (right) conjugation action** `g \star h := \hat\sigma(g)\, h\, g^{-1}`. This is
a genuine left `G`-action on itself: `(g_1 g_2) \star h = \hat\sigma(g_1 g_2) h (g_1g_2)^{-1}
= \hat\sigma(g_1)\big(\hat\sigma(g_2) h g_2^{-1}\big) g_1^{-1} = g_1 \star (g_2 \star h)`, using that
`\hat\sigma` is a homomorphism. The identity above reads exactly:

> **`c` is constant on every σ̂-twisted conjugacy orbit `\{g \star h : g \in G\}`.**

(This reproduces the freeze §2 D0-iii statement `c_{σ̂(g)⁻¹k} = c_{kg⁻¹}` exactly, independently
re-derived here.)

### The ℓ² obstruction

Since `\sum_h |c_h|^2 = 1 < \infty`, and `c` is constant on each twisted orbit: if some orbit
`\{g \star h_0 : g \in G\}` is **infinite** and `c_{h_0} \neq 0`, then `\sum_{h \in \text{orbit}}
|c_h|^2 = |c_{h_0}|^2 \cdot |\text{orbit}| = \infty`, contradicting `\ell^2` summability. Hence:

> **If every σ̂-twisted conjugacy class is infinite, then `c \equiv 0`, so `u = 0` — contradicting
> `\|u\|_2 = 1`. Hence no such `u` exists: `α` is not inner.**

### Proving "no finite σ̂-twisted class" for THIS σ̂ (word-length growth, twisted version)

Fix any `h \in G`, with reduced word (possibly empty) of syllable factors `j_1` (first) and `j_k`
(last, if `h \neq e`). We exhibit an explicit infinite family of pairwise-distinct elements of `h`'s
twisted orbit, by the **same technique as D0-i**, now twisted by `σ̂`.

Choose `b \in \{1,\dots,6\}` with `b \notin \{\sigma^{-1}(j_1),\, j_k\}` (at most 2 forbidden values,
so ≥ 4 valid choices among 6 generators; if `h = e` there is **no** forbidden value from `h` at all —
see below), and any `a \neq b`. Set `g_n = (t_a t_b)^n` (reduced length `2n`). Since `σ̂` is a
**bijective relabelling of the generator alphabet** (a group automorphism induced by the permutation
`σ`), `\hat\sigma(g_n) = (t_{\sigma(a)} t_{\sigma(b)})^n`, still reduced (as `σ(a) \neq σ(b)`, `σ`
injective), length `2n`. Consider `w_n = \hat\sigma(g_n)\, h\, g_n^{-1}`:

- **Left junction:** last letter of `\hat\sigma(g_n)` is `t_{\sigma(b)}`; first syllable of `h` is
  `t_{j_1}`; the choice `b \neq \sigma^{-1}(j_1)` means `\sigma(b) \neq j_1` ⟹ no cancellation.
- **Right junction:** last syllable of `h` is `t_{j_k}`; first letter of `g_n^{-1} = (t_b t_a)^n` is
  `t_b`; the choice `b \neq j_k` ⟹ no cancellation.
- No internal cancellation (alternating words in two distinct generators, at each end of `w_n`).

So `|w_n| = 2n + |h| + 2n = 4n + |h|` **exactly**, and the `w_n` (`n=0,1,2,\dots`) are pairwise
distinct: `h`'s σ̂-twisted class is infinite.

**The case `h = e` is the one that needs `σ` fixed-point-free, and this is exactly where it is used.**
For `h = e`, `j_1, j_k` don't exist, so the only requirement is that the single junction (`\hat\sigma(g_n)`
meeting `g_n^{-1}` directly) not cancel: last letter of `\hat\sigma(g_n)` is `t_{\sigma(b)}`, first
letter of `g_n^{-1}` is `t_b`; no cancellation requires `\sigma(b) \neq b`. Since **`σ` is
fixed-point-free on all of `{1,\dots,6}`** (verified directly: `σ=(1\,2\,3)(4\,5\,6)` moves every
point), **this holds for every `b`, with no exception to search for.** This is precisely why the
freeze's D0-ii already having established `σ` fixed-point-free (needed there just for "moves
generators across factors") turns out to be load-bearing again here, for the identity's own twisted
class — the one orbit that could not use the "avoid `h`'s own boundary factors" trick because `e` has
no boundary syllables at all.

Since `h` was arbitrary, **every σ̂-twisted conjugacy class is infinite.** By the ℓ² obstruction above,
`u = 0`, contradiction. **Hence `α \notin \mathrm{Inn}(M)`.**

### "Not inner on a factor" ⟹ "properly outer"

**Fact (standard; special to factors).** For a single automorphism `α` of a von Neumann algebra `M`,
`α` is called *properly outer* if there is no nonzero `α`-invariant central projection `p` such that
`\alpha|_{pMp}` is inner on the corner `pMp`; in general this is strictly stronger than "not globally
inner." **But when `M` is a factor**, the only central projections are `0` and `1`, so there is no room
for a nontrivial corner: `α` not inner on `M` (`= 1 \cdot M \cdot 1`) **is already** the statement that
no nonzero invariant central projection admits an inner restriction. Hence **on a factor, "not inner"
and "properly outer" coincide** (see e.g. Jones, *Actions of finite groups on the hyperfinite type II₁
factor*, Mem. AMS 1980, or Connes, *Outer conjugacy classes of automorphisms of factors*, Ann. Sci.
ENS 1975, for the standard definitions and this elementary factor-case collapse).

Since `M = L(G)` is a factor (D0-i) and `α` is not inner (above), **`α` is properly outer on `M`.**
`∎ (D0-iii)`

This disposes of the freeze's risk-flag #1 (proper vs. plain outerness collapsing on a factor) by
stating the fact cleanly with citation, as instructed.
