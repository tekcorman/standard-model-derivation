## 1. Theorem statement

**Theorem (Multiway Branch Measure).** Under A1 + A2-T (waterline thm; refined A2) and the four cited upstream results (§2), the multiway DAG generated by A1's toggle substrate carries a canonical probability measure μ with the following properties:

**(P1) Definition.** On the space Σ* = ∪_{L ≥ 0} E^L of finite toggle sequences (where E is A1's toggle alphabet), μ is the uniform product measure:

$$\mu(B) = |E|^{-L} \qquad \text{for any specific length-}L\text{ sequence } B = (e_1, \ldots, e_L) \in E^L.$$

**(P2) Admissibility.** The push-forward of μ under A2's canonicalization map Π: Σ* → F_inv(E) is supported on reduced words (NB walks on the Cayley graph of F_inv(E)). The probability that a length-L initial segment is admissible (reduced) is

$$\mu(\text{reduced of length } L) = \frac{|E|(|E|-1)^{L-1}}{|E|^L} = \frac{(|E|-1)^{L-1}}{|E|^{L-1}} = \left(\frac{|E|-1}{|E|}\right)^{L-1}$$

on a k-regular substrate where |E| enumerates directed-edge types at each vertex (k-regular: |E| = 2k around a single vertex; directed on the quotient: k choices per step under NB admissibility).

**(P3) Multiplicative compounding.** For any event A ⊆ Σ* that is a product of step-wise events A = ∏_i A_i with A_i ⊆ E, μ(A) = ∏_i μ_1(A_i) where μ_1(e) = 1/|E|.

**(P4) Conditional uniformity on admissible continuations.** Given admissibility, the conditional measure at each step is uniform over the (k−1) non-backtracking continuations on a k-regular substrate.

**(P5) A3-consistency.** μ's marginal on single directed edges is the uniform stationary distribution of the NB random walk (Levin-Peres-Wilmer 2009 Thm 1.14). This marginal equals the partial trace, over a "history register" H_aux, of a pure state |ψ⟩ on H_visible ⊗ H_aux whose reduced-word amplitudes are determined by μ. (Explicit |ψ⟩ construction in §8.)

**(P6) Uniqueness.** Any probability measure on Σ* satisfying (P1) step-wise symmetry under A1's toggle-relabeling automorphisms, (P3) multiplicativity along branches, and (P4) admissibility conditioning equals μ.

## 3. Proof of (P1): definition of μ

**Claim.** On Σ* = ∪_L E^L, the measure μ(B) = |E|^{-L} for B ∈ E^L is uniquely determined by A1 + A2-T + the cited Grunwald-Jaynes upstream.

**Proof.**

**3.1.** Under A1, every toggle symbol e ∈ E is characterized by the same relation T_e · T_e = I. No toggle symbol is structurally distinguished from another by A1's content alone; A1's group-theoretic structure F_inv(E) is equivariant under the symmetric group S_{|E|} permuting toggle labels.

**3.2.** Under A2 (refined), the observer's state-identification cost is the two-part MDL code length L_total = L_model + L_data|model (Rissanen 1978/1983; Grunwald 2007 §5.1). By the MDL-Bayes duality theorem (Grunwald 2007 §5.2-5.3, building on Barron-Rissanen-Yu 1998): the MDL code-assignment is equivalent to a Bayesian posterior under a prior π over the model class, where π is the universal (Bayes-mixture-over-codes-reaching-R(D)) prior.

**3.3.** The prior π on the toggle alphabet E must respect the S_{|E|} symmetry of 3.1 — any π that distinguishes specific labels would require structural content A1 does not supply. By a standard symmetry argument (equivalent to Jaynes 1957's maximum-entropy-under-constraints theorem applied to a finite alphabet with no non-symmetric constraints): the unique S_{|E|}-invariant probability distribution on E is uniform, π(e) = 1/|E| for all e.

**3.4.** μ is then constructed as the product measure on Σ* = ∪_L E^L with per-symbol marginal π: for B = (e_1, ..., e_L), μ(B) = ∏_i π(e_i) = |E|^{-L}. The product-measure construction is standard Kolmogorov.

**3.5.** The independence across steps required for 3.4 is supplied by A1's substrate structure: F_inv(E)'s word operation is position-independent (toggles compose freely, only adjacent-cancellation relations apply), so at the pre-canonicalization level, toggle applications at distinct positions are structurally independent. Shalizi-Crutchfield 2001 Theorem 2 then ensures that after canonicalization (passing to the MDL-optimal representation), the causal state at each step is the current directed edge, making the Markov property rigorous.

**Therefore** μ is uniquely defined as the uniform product measure on Σ*, with μ(B) = |E|^{-L} for B ∈ E^L. ∎

**Remark on load-bearing step.** Step 3.2 invokes Grunwald 2007's MDL-Bayes duality to move from A2's selection-rule form to a probability-weight form. This is the critical bridge. Grunwald's result is a published theorem; invoking it here is a gate-type-3 step (cited theorem) under parameter_linter.md. Users skeptical of this bridge — specifically, skeptical that A2's selection-rule content CAN be mapped to a probability weighting without additional axiom content — must escalate to Option C (strengthen A2 to carry the weighting explicitly) or Option D (add A6 naming μ). ## 4. Proof of (P2) and (P3): admissibility and compounding

**Claim (P2).** μ's push-forward under A2's canonicalization map Π is supported on reduced words, with measure on length-L initial reduced segments equal to (|E|·(|E|−1)^{L−1})/|E|^L on k-regular substrate where k = |E|/2 (undirected edges) or |E| (directed edges).

**Proof.**

**4.1.** By Serre 1980 §I.1 Prop 4, every word w ∈ E* has a unique reduced representative r(w) ∈ F_inv(E). The canonicalization map Π sends w ↦ r(w).

**4.2.** Under A2 refined, the observer's state-representation is the canonical (reduced-word) form. By `predictions/walker_dynamics_derivation.md` Step 2 + Grunwald 2007 §5.1-5.3, this representation IS the MDL-optimal observer's data.

**4.3.** On a k-regular Cayley graph (|E| = k types of directed edges or 2k when accounting for reverse orientations, per the framework's conventions; k = 3 for srs), an initial length-L reduced segment is constructed by: first step, any of |E| choices (k choices on directed quotient); each subsequent step, (k−1) non-backtracking continuations out of k possible continuations. The number of length-L reduced segments is |E|·(|E|−1)^{L−1} (using directed-edge conventions where |E| = k per starting vertex).

**4.4.** By (P1), each specific length-L sequence in Σ* has μ-weight |E|^{-L}. Summing over admissible (reduced) length-L segments:

$$\mu(\text{reduced of length } L) = \frac{|E| \cdot (|E|-1)^{L-1}}{|E|^L} = \left(\frac{|E|-1}{|E|}\right)^{L-1}.$$

**4.5.** On k = 3 srs directed-edge structure: admissibility survival at length L is ((k−1)/k)^{L−1} = (2/3)^{L−1}. This is the NB-walk survival content used throughout the framework. ∎

**Claim (P3).** Multiplicative compounding of branch probabilities is inherited from the product-measure structure of (P1).

**Proof.** By (P1), μ is a product measure across steps. For any event A ⊆ Σ* that factorizes across steps as A = ∏_i A_i, Kolmogorov's product-measure construction gives μ(A) = ∏_i μ_1(A_i). This includes the admissibility event: admissibility-at-step-i factorizes over i, so μ(admissible of length L) = ∏_i μ_1(admissible at step i | causal state). ∎

## 5. Proof of (P4): conditional uniformity

**Claim.** Given admissibility (reduction), the conditional distribution at each step is uniform on the (k−1) NB continuations.

**Proof.** At step i, the causal state (Shalizi-Crutchfield 2001 Thm 2) is the current directed edge e_i. The k possible continuations are indexed by the k toggle symbols; one of these is the reverse of e_i (backtracking; excluded by admissibility). The remaining (k−1) continuations are admissible. By (P1)'s uniform per-symbol marginal restricted to the admissible subset (which is the (k−1)-element complement of backtracking), the conditional is uniform: π(e_{i+1} | admissible) = 1/(k−1) for each of the (k−1) admissible continuations. ∎

## 6. Corollary 1 — Exponent Principle / α₁_bare from μ

**Claim.** μ applied to the girth-2 NB-walk-survival event class on 3-regular srs (girth g = 10) equals α₁_bare = (2/3)^8.

**Proof.**

The girth-2 event class is the set of admissible length-(g−2) walk segments: initial edge e_1 fixed, (g−2) intermediate admissible steps, final edge constrained to close onto e_1's g-cycle.

By (P2), the probability of (g−2) intermediate admissible continuations is ((k−1)/k)^{g−2} = (2/3)^8 on k = 3 srs with g = 10.

The closure constraint at step g is satisfied with probability 1 given the preceding g−2 admissible steps on a girth-g closed cycle (the cycle is completed by the (g−1)-th step following the closure pattern of the cycle topology; this is the feature of girth-g cycles on srs — `predictions/g_girth.py`).

Therefore μ(girth-2 NB-walk survival) = (2/3)^8 = α₁_bare. ∎

**Numerical check.** (2/3)^8 = 256/6561 ≈ 0.039018. This matches `predictions/feshbach_exponent_principle.py`'s output at machine precision. ✓

**Significance.** The Feshbach Exponent Principle is recovered as a specific μ-moment. What was previously a STRICT-SOLID identification in its own right is now a Corollary of μ's construction. No independent axiom content.

**Proof sketch.**

**7.1.** The Hashimoto operator B on the directed-edge space (of dimension 2|E| per primitive cell) is defined by B[e', e] = 1 iff e → e' is a valid NB transition, 0 otherwise (Hashimoto 1989; `predictions/walker_dynamics_derivation.md` Step 6).

**7.2.** The normalized step-transition matrix of the walker is B_norm = B/(k−1), which is row-stochastic on the admissible continuations. Under μ (with per-step uniform on admissibles, (P4)), B_norm is the single-step transition kernel.

**7.3.** At Bloch momentum k_P (the Ramanujan point), B(P) has eigenvalues h = (√3 + i√5)/2 with multiplicity 2, h̄ with multiplicity 2, and ±1 at the "trivial" C_3-fixed sector (totaling the (4,2,2) multiplicities; `../../predictions/B_P_doubly_degenerate_h_derivation.md`).

**7.4.** |h|² = k−1 = 2 is the per-step probability content of the walker at k_P — this is μ's survival content saturated at the Ramanujan bound (Alon 1986 Ramanujan inequality becomes equality).

**7.5.** arg(h) = arctan(√5/√3) is the walker's per-step phase accumulation, which is NOT a probability — it is the orientation content inherited from the directed-edge structure of the NB walk.

**7.6.** Under A5(a)'s identification (Ramanujan eigenvalues = SM mass spectrum), the mass spectrum is uniquely determined by μ's closed-cycle spectral content + the walker's directional phase structure. The scalar pairing theorem shows this identification has a unique consistent form under A1-A4; under the reframing, that uniqueness is the proof of A5(a) as a theorem about μ rather than an independent axiom about eigenvalues.

## 8. Proof of (P5): A3-consistency

**Claim.** μ's single-edge marginal equals the partial trace, over a history auxiliary, of a pure state on H_visible ⊗ H_aux.

**Proof.**

**8.1.** By Levin-Peres-Wilmer 2009 Theorem 1.14, the NB random walk on an irreducible finite directed graph has a unique uniform stationary distribution on directed edges. Under μ, the walker's distribution at time t ≫ mixing-time converges to this uniform stationary distribution on the 2|E|-dimensional directed-edge space.

**8.2.** A3 (`../framework/framework_axioms.md` §4) asserts: every visible-sector mixed state ρ on H_visible is the partial trace of a pure state |ψ⟩ on H_visible ⊗ H_aux for some abstract purifying auxiliary H_aux.

**8.3.** Specify |ψ⟩ explicitly for the walker's stationary state. Let H_aux = ℓ²(histories) = ℓ²(Σ*) be the Hilbert space with orthonormal basis {|h⟩ : h ∈ Σ*}. Define

$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_{h \in \Sigma^*} \sqrt{\mu(h)} \cdot e^{i\phi(h)} \cdot |e_{\text{current}}(h)\rangle \otimes |h\rangle,$$

where e_{current}(h) is the current directed edge after history h, and φ(h) is a phase function determined by the walker's Bloch decomposition (for srs at k_P: φ(h) accumulates arg(h_{Ramanujan}) per step along the walk, consistent with §7.5).

**8.4.** Normalization: Σ_h μ(h) = 1 (probability normalization of μ per (P1)+(P2)), so ⟨ψ|ψ⟩ = 1. ✓

**8.5.** Tracing out H_aux:

$$\rho_{\text{visible}} = \text{Tr}_{H_{\text{aux}}}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|) = \sum_h \mu(h) \cdot |e_{\text{current}}(h)\rangle\langle e_{\text{current}}(h)|.$$

**8.6.** The coefficient of |e⟩⟨e| in ρ_visible is Σ_{h: e_{current}(h) = e} μ(h) = μ(\text{walker at edge } e) = 1/(2|E|) for stationary μ (by Step 8.1). So ρ_visible = (1/(2|E|)) I_{H_visible} = uniform stationary density operator. ✓

**8.7.** This construction is the standard GNS / purification construction (Ohya-Petz 2004 §2.5) applied to the walker's canonical ensemble. It provides an explicit realization of A3's H_aux as the walker's history register, with |ψ⟩ the "Schrödinger state" over all histories.

**Therefore** μ is consistent with A3: μ's visible-marginal equals the A3-purification's partial trace. ∎

**Remark.** This construction identifies A3's abstract H_aux with the walker's history space. The framework's earlier version of A3 (pre-2026-04-17) identified H_aux with H_dark as a concrete subspace; the 2026-04-17 revision made H_aux abstract. Under this theorem, the abstract H_aux has a natural concrete realization — the history register ℓ²(Σ*) of toggle sequences — although this realization is not forced by A3 (essential uniqueness clause of CDP 2011 allows any reversible redefinition of H_aux). This is a useful identification for understanding A3 concretely, not a new axiom.

## 9. Proof of (P6): uniqueness

**Claim.** Any probability measure ν on Σ* satisfying (a) S_{|E|}-symmetry under A1's toggle-relabeling, (b) multiplicativity along branches, (c) uniform conditioning on admissible continuations, equals μ.

**Proof sketch.** Symmetry (a) + multiplicativity (b) force ν to be a product measure with S_{|E|}-symmetric per-step marginal. The only S_{|E|}-symmetric probability distribution on a finite alphabet E is uniform (Jaynes 1957). Therefore ν's per-step marginal is 1/|E| on each symbol. By (b), ν(B) = |E|^{-L} for any B of length L. This is μ by (P1). ∎
