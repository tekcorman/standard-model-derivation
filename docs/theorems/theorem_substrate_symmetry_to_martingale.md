## Question

The existing martingale result (`../forward_constructions/forward_construction_substrate_martingales.md` §2) establishes a Bayesian-posterior martingale on the substrate observation filtration under **time-translation** invariance of the toggle Markov chain, identifying this as the substrate's analog of Noether's theorem for energy conservation.

§5.1 of that document flags three further candidate symmetries — spatial translation, C₃ cyclic shift, Pati-Salam embedding — as "forward-construction follow-ups," but does not state a *general* theorem under which each candidate would land as a corollary.

**The investigation question:** Is there a single substrate-level statement from which conservation under any continuous (or discrete) symmetry of the substrate's prior + observation filtration follows as a corollary?

If yes: the framework's Noether-analog is one theorem with many corollaries — each open parameter potentially attackable by identifying the right symmetry, rather than by direct numerical computation.

This document answers the question.

---

## Result (preview)

**Theorem (Substrate Symmetry → Martingale → Conservation).** *Let G be a group acting measurably on the substrate state space, with*
1. *a G-invariant prior π_0 over the model class 𝒬,*
2. *a G-equivariant observation filtration {𝒢_n}, and*
3. *a G-invariant model functional f : 𝒬 → ℝ.*

*Then the running posterior expectation E_{π_n}[f(Q)] is a {𝒢_n}-martingale, and its expected value at every n equals E_{π_0}[f(Q)].*

The strong corollary is the second clause: **the value of the conserved quantity is fixed by the symmetry alone** (= prior expectation, determined by G-invariance of π_0 plus G-invariance of f). For continuous G with infinitesimal generator T, this means every f in ker(T) gives a conserved expectation whose value is symmetry-determined.

Three corollary stubs are recorded in §5: time-translation reproduces the existing result; spatial translation, C₃ cyclic shift, and Pati-Salam embedding are flagged as Phase 1 corollary deliverables.

**Net structural finding:** the substrate's Noether-analog is a single theorem on G-invariant priors with G-invariant functionals; specific symmetry corollaries reduce to verifying the three hypotheses (G-invariance of π_0, G-equivariance of {𝒢_n}, G-invariance of f) for the symmetry in question.

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 Substrate observation filtration

Per A1 + A2-T (`theorem_A2_mdl_from_finite_register.md`), with P1' a derived theorem under A1 (`theorem_p1_prime_derived_from_a1.md`), the substrate is F_inv(E)'s Cayley graph with an observation filtration {𝒢_n}_{n ∈ ℕ}:

- **𝒢_0** = trivial σ-algebra.
- **𝒢_n** = σ-algebra generated by substrate observations through time step n.
- Property: 𝒢_n ⊂ 𝒢_{n+1}.

For multiway / non-backtracking-walk variants, "time step" is replaced by "NB-walk extension by one step." The filtration property holds identically.

### 1.2 Model class and prior

Per A2-T Step 8 (`theorem_A2_mdl_from_finite_register.md` §11):

- **𝒬** = model class.
- **π_0(Q)** = prior over models from A2-T's plural-retention rule (uniform over above-waterline; weighted by compression savings).
- **π_n(Q) = π(Q | x_1, …, x_n)** = posterior after n observations.

### 1.3 Group action

Let G be a group (continuous or discrete) acting measurably on substrate states. The action induces:

- **σ_g : 𝒬 → 𝒬** on the model class (push-forward of the action on substrate states).
- **σ_g : F → F** on the observation σ-algebra.
- **Time-shift τ : G → ℤ** (possibly trivial) recording how the G-action shifts observation indices.

**Definition (G-invariant prior).** π_0 is G-invariant iff π_0(σ_g · Q) = π_0(Q) for all g ∈ G, Q ∈ 𝒬.

**Definition (G-equivariant filtration).** {𝒢_n} is G-equivariant iff σ_g(𝒢_n) ⊆ 𝒢_{n + τ(g)} for all g, n. When τ ≡ 0 the symmetry preserves the filtration (e.g., spatial translation does not shift the observation clock); when τ(g) = g (G = ℤ acting by time shift) we recover the time-translation case.

**Definition (G-invariant functional).** f : 𝒬 → ℝ is G-invariant iff f(σ_g · Q) = f(Q) for all g, Q. For continuous G with infinitesimal generator T acting on functionals by Lie derivative, this is equivalent to T·f = 0.

## 2. Main theorem

**Theorem 1 (Substrate Symmetry → Conservation).** *Suppose:*

*(H1) π_0 is G-invariant.*
*(H2) {𝒢_n} is G-equivariant.*
*(H3) f : 𝒬 → ℝ is G-invariant and bounded.*

*Then the process M_n := E_{π_n}[f(Q)] is a martingale with respect to {𝒢_n} under π_0, and*

$$\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[M_n] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[f(Q)] \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0.$$

The first conclusion (martingale property) is information-theoretic; the second conclusion (symmetry-determined value) is the strong Noether-analog: **the conserved expectation's value is forced by symmetry alone.**

---

## 3. Proof

The proof composes two standard pieces — Doob's martingale theorem on Bayesian posteriors and an invariance argument for the value — with no new probability theory.

**Step 1 (martingale property — Williams 1991 §10.7).** For *any* prior π_0 (no symmetry hypothesis required), the running posterior π_n is a martingale w.r.t. {𝒢_n} under π_0:

$$\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[\pi_{n+1}(Q) \mid \mathcal{G}_n] = \pi_n(Q)$$

This is Doob's martingale (Williams 1991 §10.7). For any bounded f, the running posterior expectation E_{π_n}[f(Q)] inherits the martingale property by linearity.

So the martingale property of M_n holds without (H1), (H2), or (H3). What hypotheses (H1)–(H3) buy is the *value* of the conserved quantity.

**Step 2 (symmetry-determined value).** Because π_0 is G-invariant (H1), for any G-invariant functional f (H3),

$$\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[f(Q)] \;=\; \int f(Q) \, d\pi_0(Q) \;=\; \int f(\sigma_g \cdot Q) \, d\pi_0(\sigma_g \cdot Q) \;=\; \int f(Q) \, d\pi_0(Q),$$

so E_{π_0}[f(Q)] is well-defined and constant in g. By the martingale property (Step 1), E_{π_0}[M_n] = E_{π_0}[M_0] = E_{π_0}[f(Q)] for all n. Hypothesis (H2) ensures the conditional structure E_{π_0}[· | 𝒢_n] is consistent under the G-action, so the martingale identity respects G-invariance for all n. ∎

**Remark on Step 2.** The proof shows that **(H1) + (H3) alone determine the *value*** of the conserved expectation; (H2) is needed only to make the conditioning compatible with the G-action across filtration levels. In the time-translation case (H2) is automatic; in spatial-translation it requires checking that observations carry equivariantly under primitive-cell translations.

## 4. Corollary structure

Theorem 1 reduces "prove conservation under symmetry G" to the three-point checklist:

1. **Verify (H1):** does π_0 inherit G-invariance from A2-T's plural-retention rule? (For most natural G, yes — π_0 weights by compression savings, which is symmetry-invariant. Needs explicit verification per symmetry.)
2. **Verify (H2):** is the observation filtration G-equivariant? (For symmetries that act on substrate states without shifting the observation clock, equivariance reduces to commutativity of σ_g with the σ-algebra refinement.)
3. **Identify G-invariant functionals f:** these are the candidates for "conserved quantities." For continuous G, f ∈ ker(T).

When all three hold, the conserved expectation has its value forced by symmetry.

## 5. Corollaries

### 5.1 Corollary 1 — time-translation (recovers existing result)

**Statement.** For G = ℤ acting by time shift on the toggle Markov chain (constant p_create=1/2, p_destroy=1/3), every bounded model functional f gives a conserved running posterior expectation.

**Verification of hypotheses:**
- **(H1) π_0 invariant under time shift:** A2-T's prior weights only compression savings, which is time-shift invariant for a stationary Markov chain. ✓
- **(H2) {𝒢_n} time-equivariant:** the time shift carries 𝒢_n to 𝒢_{n+1} by construction. ✓
- **(H3) f time-invariant:** *every* bounded f is time-invariant in the sense required (its functional form does not depend on n). ✓

**Conclusion.** Theorem 1 reproduces `../forward_constructions/forward_construction_substrate_martingales.md` §2 verbatim. The time-translation case is the corollary in which (H3) is trivially universal; the proof there is the special case of the proof here.

**Status:** **Theorem-grade**, identical to the existing forward-construction result.

## 6. Inverse Noether — symmetry discovery from observed martingales

Theorem 1 has a partial converse worth recording explicitly because it is *unique to the observer-layer formulation* and unavailable in standard QFT.

**Observation (inverse Noether).** If a substrate observable F is found numerically to satisfy E_{π_n}[F] = const (martingale property with constant value), the most parsimonious explanation under A2-T is that there exists a symmetry G of the (prior, filtration) pair under which F is invariant.

This is **not** a theorem (it is a parsimony argument: a martingale could in principle arise from non-symmetry causes), but it is a usable heuristic: empirical martingales on the substrate flag candidate hidden symmetries.

**Operational tool.** A scanner that surveys substrate observables for empirical-martingale behavior is a *symmetry-discovery instrument*. Each detected martingale points to a candidate symmetry; the Phase-0 theorem then tells us how to verify the symmetry (check (H1)–(H3)) and what it conserves.

**Standard QFT has no analog** because Noether's theorem there flows symmetry → current with no observer-layer machinery to run the converse. Here, because conservation is a property of an observer's running expectation, the empirical signature can be checked directly on observation data.

**Status:** **Heuristic** (parsimony, not theorem). Phase 4 of the program builds the scanner.

## 7. Honest scope

1. **The proof is a composition of standard pieces.** Doob's martingale + G-invariance of priors are classical. The novelty here is *interpretive*: identifying this composition as the substrate's Noether-analog and isolating the three-hypothesis checklist as the operational tool.

2. **The martingale property holds without symmetry.** Step 1 of the proof uses no symmetry hypothesis; π_n is a Bayesian martingale under any prior. Symmetry enters only in *fixing the value* of the conserved expectation (Step 2). The "Noether-analog" framing is justified by this value-fixing role.

3. **(H2) equivariance is the most fragile hypothesis.** For each candidate symmetry, the observer's measurement protocol must commute with the G-action. Time-translation makes this automatic; spatial translation reduces to "no fixed origin"; C₃ reduces to "symmetric treatment of generators"; Pati-Salam reduces to invariance of the Spin(6) measurement structure under the embedded subgroup.

4. **No specific QFT prediction emerges from Theorem 1 alone.** Specific predictions arise from corollaries; each corollary requires identifying the relevant G-invariant functional and showing it picks out a SM observable.

5. **Discrete symmetries (e.g., CP) are included.** The proof does not require G continuous; the Lie-derivative form (T·f = 0) is the continuous specialization of the general (σ_g · f = f for all g) condition. CP-invariance of π_0 + CP-invariance of θ_QCD as a model functional → conserved expectation, with value = E_{π_0}[θ_QCD] = 0 by CP-conjugation symmetry.

6. **Inverse Noether is a heuristic.** §6's converse is parsimony, not theorem. A numerical martingale guarantees a candidate symmetry, not a unique one. The forward direction (symmetry → conservation) remains the load-bearing one.

7. **The three corollary stubs (§5.2–§5.4) require separate Phase 1 work.** This document does not close them; it provides the engine under which they will close.
