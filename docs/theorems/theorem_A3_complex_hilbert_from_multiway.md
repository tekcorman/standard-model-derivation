## 4. Step 1 — F_inv(E) is a discrete countable group with reversible action

**Claim.** F_inv(E) is a discrete countable group. F_inv(E) acts on itself by left multiplication; this action is reversible and is generated by the involutive toggle operators of A1.

**Proof.** Every element of F_inv(E) is a finite reduced word over the finite alphabet E. The set of finite words on a finite alphabet is countable, so F_inv(E) is countable. Discreteness is automatic for countable sets with no further topology imposed.

By A1, each generator e ∈ E satisfies T_e² = id, so T_e is its own inverse. Compositions of generators (with the relations e · e = ε giving cancellation) form an associative structure with identity (the empty word ε); every element h = e₁ · e₂ · ... · e_n has inverse h⁻¹ = e_n · e_{n-1} · ... · e₁ (reverse the order, apply each involution again to cancel). Hence F_inv(E) is a group.

For any reduced word h ∈ F_inv(E), the left-multiplication map L_h: F_inv(E) → F_inv(E) defined by g ↦ red(h · g) is a bijection (its inverse is L_{h⁻¹}). The group action of F_inv(E) on itself by left multiplication is therefore reversible.

**Type 1** (A1) + **Type 3** (Serre 1980 §I.1 Prop 4 — reduced word uniqueness; standard group-theoretic action of a group on itself).

## 5. Step 2 — Natural Hilbert space is L²(F_inv(E)) with counting measure

**Claim.** The natural Hilbert space on which F_inv(E) acts is L²(F_inv(E)) with counting measure: square-summable functions on F_inv(E) with inner product ⟨f, g⟩ = Σ_{x ∈ F_inv(E)} f*(x) g(x).

**Proof.** F_inv(E) is a discrete countable group (Step 1). Every locally compact group has a left-invariant Haar measure; for discrete groups, this is the counting measure (each element has weight 1).

L²(F_inv(E); μ_counting) is the Hilbert space of complex-valued (or real-valued; field selection happens later) functions f: F_inv(E) → 𝔽 (𝔽 = ℝ or ℂ) with Σ_x |f(x)|² < ∞, equipped with inner product ⟨f, g⟩ = Σ_x f̄(x) g(x).

The basis is the family of indicator functions {δ_g : g ∈ F_inv(E)} where δ_g(h) = 1 if h = g and 0 otherwise. Inner products: ⟨δ_g, δ_h⟩ = δ_{g,h} (Kronecker), so {δ_g} is an orthonormal basis.

**Type 3** — Folland 1999 §11.1 (Haar measure on locally compact groups; counting measure on discrete groups) + §11.4 (L² spaces and their canonical bases).

## 6. Step 3 — Left regular representation is unitary

**Claim.** The left regular representation L: F_inv(E) → U(L²(F_inv(E))) defined by L_h δ_g = δ_{h·g} (extended linearly) is a unitary representation of F_inv(E).

**Proof.** Compute inner-product preservation:

$$\langle L_h \delta_g, L_h \delta_{g'} \rangle = \langle \delta_{hg}, \delta_{hg'} \rangle = \delta_{hg, hg'}.$$

Since L_h is a bijection (Step 1), $hg = hg' \iff g = g'$. So $\delta_{hg, hg'} = \delta_{g, g'} = \langle \delta_g, \delta_{g'} \rangle$. L_h preserves inner products on basis vectors; by linear extension, on all of L².

L_h has L_{h⁻¹} as its inverse, and (L_h)* = L_{h⁻¹} (where * is the L² adjoint), so L_h is unitary.

**Type 3** — Folland 1999 §11.4 (unitarity of the regular representation of a locally compact group under Haar measure; standard result).

## 7. Step 4 — Continuous-time limit exists; rapid decay closes the continuum gap

**Claim.** The discrete-time multiway evolution (one toggle generator applied per Planck step) admits a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary limit U: ℝ → 𝒰(L²(F_inv(E))).

**Proof.** The discrete-time multiway evolution is structurally a *quantum walk* on F_inv(E)'s Cayley graph: at each Planck step, a generator e ∈ E is applied (with some probability distribution over E from the substrate's intrinsic structure), and the substrate state evolves by U_e = L_e (left multiplication by e, unitary by Step 3).

The continuum limit of such a discrete-time quantum walk is established by standard quantum-walk limit theorems, given two preconditions:

**(i) Bounded vertex degree.** F_inv(E)'s Cayley graph has vertex degree |E|: each vertex has |E| outgoing edges, one per generator. By A1, |E| is finite. ✓

**(ii) Sub-step correlation length.** From Stage 3 (`theorem_lorentz_causal_sector.md` §3), per-edge connected n-point correlations decay as |C_n^conn| ≤ K · (1/6)^s on same-edge time separation s, with characteristic correlation timescale

Under (i) + (ii), Strauch 2006 establishes: the discrete-time quantum walk converges (in strong operator topology, in the appropriate scaling limit) to a continuous-time quantum walk generated by a Hamiltonian H on L²(F_inv(E)). The continuous-time quantum walk on a Cayley graph has Hamiltonian H = adjacency operator of the graph (Childs 2009).

**Type 4** (Stage 2a + Stage 3 + b1_ags_audit.py CAS verification) + **Type 3** (Strauch 2006 + Childs 2009 — quantum-walk continuum limit theorems).

## 8. Step 5 — Stone's theorem: unitary group has self-adjoint generator (in ℂ) or skew-symmetric generator (in ℝ)

**Claim.** The strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group U(t) from Step 4 has the form

- $U(t) = e^{-iHt}$ for a unique self-adjoint operator $H$ on complex L², **or**
- $U(t) = e^{Bt}$ for a unique skew-symmetric (real anti-self-adjoint) operator $B$ on real L².

**Proof.** Stone's theorem (Stone 1932 *Annals of Mathematics* 33(3), 643–648; Reed-Simon I §VIII.4) gives both versions:

- **Complex.** On a complex Hilbert space H_ℂ, every strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group U(t) has a unique self-adjoint generator A: U(t) = exp(-iAt). The "i" is essential — without it, exp(-At) for self-adjoint A would not be unitary (it would be exponentially decaying or growing).
- **Real.** On a real Hilbert space H_ℝ, every strongly continuous one-parameter orthogonal group U(t) has a unique skew-symmetric (real anti-self-adjoint, B^T = -B) generator B: U(t) = exp(Bt).

Both versions hold; Stone's theorem alone does not select between ℝ and ℂ. **Type 3** — Stone 1932; Reed-Simon I §VIII.4.

## 9. Step 6 — Spectral content distinguishes real from complex

**Claim.** The eigenvalues of the generator depend on the field:

- Skew-symmetric operators on real Hilbert spaces have purely imaginary eigenvalues (computed on the complexification).
- Self-adjoint operators on complex Hilbert spaces have real eigenvalues.

**Proof.**

**(a) Skew-symmetric on ℝ.** Let B: H_ℝ → H_ℝ satisfy B^T = -B. Compute eigenvalues on the complexification H_ℝ ⊗ ℂ. For an eigenvector v with eigenvalue λ:

$$\langle Bv, v \rangle = \lambda \langle v, v \rangle.$$

By skew-symmetry, ⟨Bv, v⟩ = ⟨v, B^T v⟩ = ⟨v, -Bv⟩ = -⟨v, Bv⟩ = -λ̄ ⟨v, v⟩ (using complex conjugate-linearity in the first slot for the complexified inner product). So:

$$\lambda \langle v, v \rangle = -\bar{\lambda} \langle v, v \rangle \implies \lambda + \bar{\lambda} = 0 \implies \text{Re}(\lambda) = 0.$$

Hence λ is purely imaginary.

**(b) Self-adjoint on ℂ.** Let A: H_ℂ → H_ℂ satisfy A* = A. For an eigenvector v with eigenvalue λ:

$$\langle Av, v \rangle = \lambda \langle v, v \rangle.$$

By self-adjointness, ⟨Av, v⟩ = ⟨v, A*v⟩ = ⟨v, Av⟩ = λ̄ ⟨v, v⟩. So λ = λ̄, i.e., λ is real.

**Type 3** — Halmos 1958 §80 (eigenvalues of self-adjoint and skew-symmetric operators on finite-dim spaces; the infinite-dim version is in Reed-Simon I §VI.2 spectral theorem) + **Type 2** (the explicit computations above).

If P1' were relaxed (e.g., allowing observers with non-finite registers, or observers that store quaternionic-valued states directly), the field selection could shift. This is structurally honest: P1' is the framework's definitional scope-fixing commitment about what an observer is. The framework's commitment to register-storable physical observables IS the commitment to complex Hilbert space — and this is now derivable without invoking the empirical mass spectrum (A5-mass), making the chain foundationally tighter than the original A5-routed version.

4. **Visible/auxiliary tensor decomposition not separately formalized.** Step 8 grounds A3's purification structure in the substrate's superposition + observer's partial-trace marginalization, leaning on `framework_axioms.md` §4's existing substrate/observer architectural distinction. A separate, more explicit identification of "visible" and "auxiliary" sub-algebras of L²(F_inv(E)) would tighten this step. Not load-bearing for the field-selection conclusion (i.e., does not affect the principal claim that the substrate's Hilbert space is complex).

5. **Continuum-limit step subsumes the gap previously held by Stage 3 Lorentz.** Stage 3 explicitly accepts the continuum limit as a working premise. The present theorem does not close that gap unconditionally — it leans on Strauch 2006 + Childs 2009 + the Stage 3 rapid-decay result, which together establish the continuum limit for the specific case of bounded-degree quantum walks on Cayley graphs. The general continuum-limit question (e.g., for the Lorentz analysis in Stage 3) remains a separate research question.
